# Quik Shopping — Pricing Plan

*Last updated: June 2026*

---

## Why This Model Exists

Quik Shopping was built to solve a trust problem: store owners with employees need to know who did what with their money, and they need to know their transfers can't be stolen by the people they hire.

The pricing model reflects that. Quik Shopping is free to use. The only fee charged is on bank transfer payments — the payment type where fraud risk is highest, and where Quik Shopping does the most work to protect the store. Cash and POS transactions cost the store nothing.

**The principle: we only earn when we're actively protecting your money.**

---

## Fee Structure

| Payment type | Fee to store | How collected |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash** | ₦0 | Not applicable |
| **POS terminal** | ₦0 | Not applicable |
| **Bank Transfer** | 1.5% of transaction value, capped at ₦500 | Automatically via Paystack / Monnify at point of payment |

There is no monthly fee, no setup fee, and no hardware cost.

---

## How the Transfer Fee Breaks Down

| | Rate | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| → Payment provider (Paystack / Monnify) | 1% | ₦300 |
| → Quik Shopping | 0.5% | ₦200 |
| **Total paid by store** | **1.5%** | **₦500** |

### Example transactions

| Sale amount | Total fee | Payment provider gets | Quik Shopping gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₦1,000 | ₦15 | ₦10 | ₦5 |
| ₦10,000 | ₦150 | ₦100 | ₦50 |
| ₦33,334+ | ₦500 (cap) | ₦300 | ₦200 |

Once a transfer exceeds ~₦33,000, the fee is always exactly ₦500. A store selling a ₦500,000 item still pays ₦500.

## How Transfer Payments Work

The traditional method for bank transfer in Nigerian retail — telling a customer to "send to this account" — is one of the most common employee fraud vectors. A dishonest cashier can give the customer their personal account number instead of the store's. The store loses the money and may not notice for days.

Quik Shopping closes this completely using **virtual account technology** via Paystack or Monnify.

When a customer pays by transfer:
1. The app generates a **one-time account number** specific to that transaction
2. The customer transfers the exact amount to that account
3. The payment provider confirms receipt automatically
4. The transaction is marked paid in the app

The store's real bank account is never shown to employees or customers. Every transfer is traceable, attributed, and logged. There is no account number to redirect.

The payment provider's fee (~1% capped at ₦300) covers this infrastructure and is included in the 1.5% total.

---

## No Monthly Fee — Why

Monthly fees punish stores during slow periods and create resistance at the point of sign-up. A store owner who is not sure yet should be able to start for free, run their POS, and decide based on experience — not based on a monthly commitment.

This also means the model is honest: Quik Shopping earns only when it is actively involved in processing a payment. Cash and POS transactions flow entirely outside the app's payment infrastructure, so there is no fee.

---

## Version 2 Pricing

When Version 2 (customer self-checkout) launches, the same 1.5% transfer fee applies to customer-initiated payments. From the store's perspective, a self-checkout sale is just another sale.

There is no additional cost for stores already on Version 1 to access Version 2.

## Competitive Context

The Nigerian POS software market includes both free and paid options:

- **Free options** (Loyverse, digabloPos) offer basic POS functionality with no monthly fee
- **Subscription options** (Vendloop ₦4,750–₦27,950/month, Simplebks ₦8,333–₦41,667/month) offer more features
- **Hardware/enterprise systems** (EPOS Solutions, starting ₦120,000+) bundle hardware with software

Quik Shopping is free at the same level as the free options, but with one capability none of them offer at this depth: **full cashier accountability**. Every sale, every product change, every action in the system is tied to a named, invited employee. Owners can see everything from their phone, in real time, from anywhere.

The transfer fee exists because Quik Shopping goes further than logging — it actively protects the payment itself.
